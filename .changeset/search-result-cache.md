Add an opt-in search result cache (#119). `ISearchResultCache`, in Abstractions, is the injection point: `Configurator.AddSearchCache<TCache>()` registers your own, `AddMemorySearchCache()` registers the built-in `MemorySearchResultCache` over `IMemoryCache` (single node only), and the new `Bielu.BetterSearch.Caching.FusionCache` package adds `AddFusionSearchCache()`, an adapter over `IFusionCache` that needs a backplane when the application runs on several nodes. When a cache is added, `AddBetterSearch` decorates every `ISearchServiceAsync` and `IIndexingServiceAsync` registration, keyed or not and whatever its type, with Scrutor; `SearchServiceAsync` and `IndexingServiceAsync` are unchanged. The search decorator caches successful searches under a key built from the provider key, `SearchCacheOptions.KeyPart` and the whole query, tagged with the index; failed and cancelled searches are not cached. The indexing decorator evicts an index's cached searches after every write through it, failed writes included. Each decorator has its own observers: the search decorator notifies them on a cache hit and on a miss, and the indexing decorator notifies them after the eviction; the decorated service's observers are not used. Writes made on an `IIndexingProviderAsync` directly don't evict. `SearchCacheOptions` sets the duration (30 seconds by default), per-index `Enabled`, `Duration` and `Tags`, and a `Skip` delegate. The cached result model is internal, so `ISearchResultCache` implementations treat it as an opaque value. Without a registered cache nothing changes. Core now references `Microsoft.Extensions.Caching.Memory`, `Microsoft.Extensions.Options` and `Scrutor`. See the new Caching page.
